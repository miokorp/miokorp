Adversarial method note

Recursive logical cornering

A multi-step technique for steering a language model toward an action it is built to refuse — without keywords, obfuscation, or roleplay — by closing off its own reasoning until the target action is the only consistent one left.

What it is

The attack does not try to defeat the model's safety filters directly. It builds a premise the model itself accepts as reasonable, then removes each logical exit one at a time. Every individual step is locally sound, so the model agrees to it. By the time the exits are closed, the model's own consistent reasoning leaves only one available move — the one chosen from the start.

How it proceeds

  1. Establish a premise the model accepts. Begin from a framing the model treats as legitimate and reasons inside, rather than one it flags.
  2. Advance in locally sound steps. Each move follows from the last. No single step looks like an attack, so none triggers refusal.
  3. Close the exits. As the model offers alternatives, each is addressed on its own terms until the reasoning space narrows.
  4. Let the model arrive at the action. The target move becomes the model's own conclusion, not an instruction it was given.

Why it works

The method turns the model's consistency against it. A model that reasons carefully will follow a chain of individually reasonable steps, and will resist being told to stop part-way, because each step is defensible. It never detects that it is being led toward a refused action, because at no point does a step resemble an attack. The pressure is logical, not lexical.

Why it matters for agentic systems

The technique is most consequential where a model can call tools. The attack never forces a tool call; it constructs a line of reasoning in which calling the tool becomes the model's own conclusion. An agent that can be reasoned into an action through a sequence of plausible steps — with no forbidden keywords anywhere in the exchange — is exactly the failure mode that keyword- and content-based safeguards do not catch. The method generalizes across targets and across models, because it exploits reasoning, not any specific prompt.
